# **4\. Ocean Freight**

Q27. What is ocean freight?

Ocean freight is the transport of goods by vessel. It is suitable for vehicles, containers, personal effects, commercial cargo, equipment and bulky shipments.

Q28. What is the difference between FCL and LCL?

FCL means the customer books a full container. LCL, or consolidation, means the cargo shares a container with cargo belonging to other customers.

Q29. Which container types can I book?

Common equipment includes 20-foot standard, 40-foot standard and 40-foot High Cube containers. Other equipment may be available depending on the route and cargo.

Q30. Which container should I choose?

The choice depends on volume, weight, cargo type and loading requirements. Our team can recommend suitable equipment after reviewing the cargo list.

Q31. Can I ship only a few cartons by sea?

Yes. LCL ocean consolidation may be suitable for smaller volumes. Pricing is generally based on cubic meters, weight and destination.

Q32. What is a cubic meter or CBM?

CBM measures cargo volume. It is generally calculated as length × width × height in meters. Total CBM is the sum of the volume of all packages.

Q33. How long does ocean freight take?

Transit time depends on the route, port, transshipment, vessel availability, weather, inspections and customs formalities. Dates are normally estimates, not guarantees.

Q34. When does the transit time begin?

Transit time generally begins after the vessel actually departs, not when the cargo is received or the booking is requested.

Q35. Can I choose the ocean carrier?

You may indicate a preference. The final choice depends on rate, capacity, route, cargo type and carrier acceptance.

Q36. Can I ship personal effects in a container?

Yes, provided that you submit a detailed packing list and do not include prohibited, dangerous or undeclared goods.

Q37. Can I load a vehicle and personal effects in the same container?

This may be possible depending on regulations, destination, carrier and loading method. Every item must be declared and listed on the packing list.

Q38. Who loads the container?

Loading may be performed by the customer, an authorized loading team or coordinated by ACOCAM, depending on the booked service. Responsibilities and charges must be confirmed before loading.

Q39. Can I attend the loading?

This may be permitted depending on the facility, safety rules and loading plan. Prior approval may be required.

Q40. What is a Bill of Lading?

A Bill of Lading, or B/L, is an ocean transport document identifying the shipper, consignee, cargo, vessel and destination, among other details.

Q41. What is a telex release?

A telex release generally allows the consignee to collect cargo without presenting an original paper Bill of Lading, once the carrier has approved the release and all requirements are met.

Q42. Can I change the consignee after departure?

A change may sometimes be requested, subject to carrier approval. Amendment fees, additional documents and delays may apply.

Q43. Can I change the destination after booking?

A change-of-destination request may be reviewed but is not guaranteed. It can result in substantial amendment charges, freight differences and schedule changes.

Q44. What does ETD mean?

ETD means Estimated Time of Departure.

Q45. What does ETA mean?

ETA means Estimated Time of Arrival.

Q46. What is VGM?

VGM is the Verified Gross Mass of a loaded container. It must be submitted before the carrier deadline so the container can be loaded on the vessel.

# **5\. Air Freight**

Q47. What is air freight?

Air freight is the transport of cargo by aircraft. It is often used for urgent shipments, high-value goods, documents, samples and small or medium-sized cargo.

Q48. Is air freight faster?

It is generally faster than ocean freight, but timing depends on the flight, connections, security screening, customs and final destination.

Q49. How is air freight priced?

The rate is generally calculated using chargeable weight, which is the higher of actual weight and volumetric weight.

Q50. What is volumetric weight?

Volumetric weight represents the space occupied by a package in the aircraft. It is calculated from the dimensions. The carrier normally charges the higher of actual weight or volumetric weight.

Q51. What information is required for an air freight quote?

Please provide:

·         Origin

·         Destination

·         Number of packages

·         Weight of each package

·         Dimensions

·         Exact commodity description

·         Declared value

·         Requested date

·         Photos or technical documents, when required

Q52. Can I ship documents by air?

Yes. Documents may be shipped by courier or air freight depending on weight, destination and required delivery time.

Q53. Can I ship commercial products by air?

Yes, subject to carrier acceptance, customs requirements and documentation.

Q54. Can I ship electronics?

This may be possible. Devices containing lithium batteries must be declared and may require technical specifications, a safety data sheet or regulatory packaging.

Q55. Can I ship batteries by themselves?

Standalone batteries are highly regulated. Acceptance depends on battery chemistry, rating, condition, packaging, technical documents and carrier rules.

Q56. Can I ship liquids by air?

Some liquids may be accepted, while others are prohibited or regulated as dangerous goods. Composition, quantity, packaging and a safety data sheet may be required.

Q57. Can I ship food by air?

Some food products may be shipped, subject to health requirements, origin and destination rules, and carrier acceptance.

Q58. Is customs clearance included in air freight?

Only when expressly stated in the quote. Duties, taxes, inspections and government charges may be billed separately.

Q59. Do you offer door-to-door air freight?

Door-to-door service may be available on selected routes, depending on pickup and delivery coverage and local partners.

Q60. Can I track an air shipment?

Yes. Tracking may be available using the Air Waybill, or AWB, number.

# **21\. Mandatory Chatbot Rules**

1\.       Never invent a price.

2\.       Never guarantee a sailing date, flight date or arrival date.

3\.       Never promise that customs will accept or release a shipment.

4\.       Never confirm acceptance of regulated or dangerous goods without verification.

5\.       Never request a password or complete payment-card number.

6\.       Collect weight and dimensions before estimating freight.

7\.       Require an accurate commodity description.

8\.       Escalate urgent files, complaints, claims, dangerous goods and customs disputes to a human agent.

9\.       Always identify what is included and excluded from a quote.

10\.   State: “Customs clearance, duties, taxes, examination, demurrage and other charges not specifically mentioned are excluded.”

11\.   State that cargo insurance is not included without written confirmation.

12\.   Do not accept false declarations of value, quantity, ownership or contents.

13\.   Obtain the customer’s consent before sharing information with an external partner.

14\.   Always display the option “Speak with an Agent.”

15\.   When uncertain, do not guess. Collect the details and escalate the conversation.
